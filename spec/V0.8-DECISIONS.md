## How the register works

### Statuses

Each entry has exactly one status, and the entry's own `Status:` line is its
record. The holdout gates that #76 (`holdout2`), #93 (the new alpha split)
and #129 (`holdout3`) build read that line, by entry ID: they find the one heading that starts with
the ID (`### D5. …`), and in that section the one line that starts
`- **Status:**` followed by the status word, plain, with no backticks. A
section with no such line, or with two, keeps the holdout sealed. The `Status`
column of the register table is a summary and must agree with the lines. Keep
the heading form, the line form and the three status words exactly as they are
written here.

| status | meaning | what may run |
|---|---|---|
| `open` | The criteria are drafted, or approved while the holdout arms are not yet fixed. | Nothing, until the entry's `Criteria approved` field is filled. After that, the entry's tune sweep. |
| `frozen` | The criteria are approved, and the arms to be scored on the holdout are fixed from the tune result by the entry's own selection rule. | The entry reads its holdout **once**, with exactly the recorded arms: `holdout3` is read once per entry (per tool, `sweep` and `rd-budget`). |
| `decided` | The verdict is recorded, with the committed result it rests on. | Nothing further under this entry. Reopening it takes a new entry. |

An entry moves forward only, `open` → `frozen` → `decided`. Two moves need the
user: filling `Criteria approved`, and the move to `frozen`, which the user
approves by approving the holdout arm list. The move to `decided` is mechanical.
It applies the frozen rule to the committed holdout result, and involves no
further judgement.

Under the delegation of 2026-11-22 ("The approval, 2026-11-22") both moves
are taken without the user, and the move to `frozen` is then mechanical too.
An entry is frozen by one commit that records, in the entry, the arm list its
own selection rule produces from its committed tune result, the freeze SHA
(the commit whose `tools/comparison` scores the holdout), and the sweep
config with its sha256. Nothing is chosen at the freeze that the selection
rule did not already fix.

Freezing an entry also pins its instrument. The freeze records the commit SHA
whose `tools/comparison` scores the holdout, and the sweep config file. The
holdout result is committed under `tools/comparison/results/` with the
provenance #73 added (git rev, corpus digests, `iqa-cli` version). A tooling
change after the freeze does not apply to a frozen entry; a change to a guard
tolerance is the example. The interval resample count #88 was to raise was
raised to 10,000 (`BOOTSTRAP_RESAMPLES`, #123) before any entry froze, so
every freeze reads intervals and p-values at the same B.

### Change classes

| class | what moves | release |
|---|---|---|
| **byte-identical** | Nothing: the same hash and the same decoded pixels. | A patch release. `spec/test-vectors/` and `mise run rd:gate` are sufficient evidence (`PERFORMANCE.md` §12.1). |
| **decode change** | The decoded pixels and the decode vectors. No hash byte changes. | A format version: the decoder is normative, so every language lands together. |
| **format change** | The hash bytes, and so every encode vector. | A format version: a version bump, regenerated vectors, and all nine languages together. |

### The decision rules

Each entry names one rule, and names which parameters it changes. Rules are
evaluated by the committed sweep tooling (`sweep.ts`, `arms-core.ts`,
`stats.ts`). Guard tolerances are the ones `sweep.ts` applies: SSIMULACRA2 may
drop at most 1.0 point (absolute), and Butteraugli and DSSIM may rise at most
2% (relative).

**R1: superiority.** This is `RATIONALE.md`'s pre-registered retune rule,
tightened to the statistics of `EXPERIMENTS.md` §13.5. An arm passes against
its incumbent, on the entry's holdout, only if all four of these hold:

1. Its mean ΔE00 is at least **3.0%** lower (point estimate).
2. The paired ΔE00 difference is significant at 0.05 **after Holm** across
   the holdout arms of that entry.
3. Every guard is **`ok` on the paired intervals** (`guards (CI)`). A guard
   that is `inconclusive` fails.
4. The verdict does not change under `mise run arms --seed-sensitivity`.
   §13.5 measured 10 Holm verdicts that change with the seed.

**R2: non-inferiority.** For candidates whose benefit is not ΔE00: speed,
decode flexibility, or artifacts. An arm passes, on the entry's holdout, if:

1. The upper bound of the paired 95% interval of mean ΔE00 (arm − incumbent)
   is at most **+δ**, where δ is **1.0%** of the incumbent's mean unless the
   entry sets it otherwise.
2. Every guard is `ok` on intervals, as in R1.
3. The entry's own primary metric passes its own threshold.
4. Condition 4 of R1.

**Selection on tune.** Each entry fixes, before its tune sweep runs, how the
holdout arms are chosen from the tune result. The default is **one arm**: the
tune arm with the best primary metric among those whose tune `guards (CI)`
verdict is not `FAIL`, scored against the incumbent. Taking at most two arms
keeps the Holm family on the holdout small. Holm is still applied across
whatever arms are recorded. Candidates are tuned on tune only, and no entry
records more than two holdout arms.

**The stratum rule.** Every `holdout3` result is reported overall and in
each of its strata: smartphone, graphics, alpha, skin, low-light and extreme
aspect (`EXPERIMENTS.md` §11.12; a pin may sit in several). The entry's rule
(R1 or R2) is applied overall, and carries the decision. An arm that passes
it overall still **fails** if, in any stratum, against the same incumbent:

1. a guard is `FAIL` on its paired 95% interval: the SSIMULACRA2 drop is
   beyond 1.0 point, or the Butteraugli or DSSIM rise is beyond 2%, as
   `guardVerdictOnIntervals` applies them; or
2. the lower bound of its paired 95% ΔE00 interval (arm − incumbent) lies
   above **+1.0%** of that stratum's incumbent mean.

A stratum whose guards are `inconclusive`, and which fails neither test, is
reported as `inconclusive` and does not fail the arm: strata have small n,
and the overall test carries the decision. A stratum the result holds no
image of is not reported. The strata are guards, not tests of a gain, so
they take no multiplicity adjustment, and condition 4 of R1 covers them: the
stratum outcome must not change under the other seeds either.
`register-verdict-core.ts` (`stratumRule`) derives it from a committed
result.

**The adaptive/conditional rule.** A gain that depends on the input is
admissible only in one of two forms:

- **adaptive:** the encoder decides it per image, and the decision is
  signalled in the hash or derivable from it, so the decoder needs nothing
  out of band;
- **conditional:** it is active only for an input class the encoder detects
  from the input and the decoder recovers from the hash, such as the tier,
  the aspect or the alpha flag.

The arm is scored as deployed: the encoder's own per-image decision, with its
signalling paid for inside the same bytes, never an oracle that picks the
better of two encodes after scoring them. A fixed setting that helps one
stratum and hurts another is not an adaptive candidate; it is judged as it
stands, under the stratum rule.

### D1. Entropy-coded AC

- **Question.** Does coding the AC codes with an adaptive entropy coder, and
  spending the bits it saves on more coefficients, beat the shipped fixed-width
  layout at equal bytes by enough to pay for the loss of the O(1) length check?
- **Class.** Format change. The length formula stops being the validity check,
  so `ChromaHash::from_bytes` needs a different guarantee that a validated hash
  decodes. An approved design for that guarantee is **required before
  freeze**: the metric cannot price the loss of the check, so passing R1 is
  necessary for adoption, but not sufficient.
- **Prior evidence (tune only).** `EXPERIMENTS.md` §7.13, with the coder scored
  leave-one-image-out: −7.5% of the AC bits at 32 B, which buys **−1.6% ΔE00**
  at code 1 and **−4.8%** at code 2. Both are against the best fixed-field
  layout at the same budget, not the shipped one. `RATIONALE.md` ("No entropy
  coding") says the trade reverses at codes 3–4, where there are 3–13 kbit, but
  nothing has measured those codes.
- **Primary metric and threshold.** R1, at equal bytes against the shipped
  layout of each tier. Each tier is decided separately.
- **Tiers.** Codes 2, 3 and 4. Code 1 is excluded because §7.13's tune estimate
  (−1.6%) is below R1's 3% before any holdout shrinkage. Code 0 is excluded
  too: its AC payload is smaller still, and §7.13 finds that the saving shrinks
  with the payload (a table-free coder loses to the static entropy at code 1's
  44 symbols).
- **The coder (#132).** `Tunables::ac_coder = 1` (`rust/src/bitpack.rs`,
  off by default) replaces the fixed-width AC fields with one stream: an
  integer binary arithmetic coder (Witten, Neal and Cleary's interval coder,
  32-bit, with the underflow rule) over 12-bit adaptive probabilities that
  start at one half and move `2^−min(n+1, 4)` of the way to the `n`-th bit
  they code (half the way on the first, 1/16 from the fourth on). It has no
  table. Each AC code `q` of a `b`-bit odd-level field, centre
  `c = 2^(b−1) − 1`, is coded as `s = q − c`: a significance bit modelled by
  channel group (luma, chroma with a and b sharing, alpha), frequency band
  `⌊log2(i + 1)⌋` of its selection index `i` (capped at 11) and how many of
  the channel's previous two codes were significant; then, for `s ≠ 0`, a
  sign at even odds and `|s| − 1` in truncated unary to `c − 1`, each bin
  modelled by group, band and bin index (bins past the fourth share one).
  The stream carries the codes channel-sequentially (L, a, b, alpha) and
  fills the hash after the fixed prefix (descriptor, aspect, DC, scales and
  the alpha DC and scale, all unchanged), so `average_color` still reads
  only the prefix. The hash length is the one the shipped layout gives the
  same tier and alpha mode, so every arm is at equal bytes per image by
  construction. The arm's layout bounds the codes the stream may carry; at
  step `k` of 256 every channel keeps its first `⌈count · k / 256⌉` codes and
  zeroes the rest, and the encoder keeps every code if that fits and
  otherwise bisects for a step at which the stream fits and the next does
  not (the coded length is not strictly monotone in `k`, so that is a
  boundary, not always the largest step that fits). Termination emits one
  decision bit and drops trailing zeros.
  `ac_coder = 2` is the **adaptive** form, which carries U13 (per-image
  signalled selection, "Not registered"): the first payload bit chooses, per
  image, the coded stream (in the bits after it) or the shipped fixed-width
  fields (one bit later than they ship; every shipped layout at codes 2–4
  leaves at least one padding bit, so they fit). The encoder writes both and
  keeps the one whose AC codes reconstruct the source's coefficients with the
  smaller Parseval-weighted squared error, `δ² · (cx > 0 ? 2 : 1) ·
  (cy > 0 ? 2 : 1)` summed over L, a, b and alpha and over every coefficient
  either selects. It is decided from the source alone and paid for inside
  the same bytes, which is what the adaptive/conditional rule asks.
- **Validity design (#132).** Design (b) of the two #132 allows: **a total
  code**. The decoder reads a fixed number of codes, which the tier, the
  alpha flag and the layout fix, and every one is in range by construction
  (the truncated unary stops at `c`). Every bit past the end of the hash
  reads as zero. The adaptive form drops its flag bit and decodes the rest
  in the mode the bit names, and both modes are total. The interval invariant `low ≤ value ≤ high` holds for any
  input bits, not only an encoder's: each decision takes the half that holds
  `value`, and each renormalisation maps all three by the same affine step.
  So **every byte string of the header-implied length decodes**, and
  `ChromaHash::from_bytes` keeps its checks exactly as they are: version,
  tier, reserved bits and the O(1) length, with no new `ChromaHashError`
  variant. Design (a), a bounded decode inside `from_bytes`, was not taken:
  it would make validation O(n) and add an error a total code cannot raise.
  What is lost is not the length check but three properties of the fixed
  fields: (1) **random access** — an AC code no longer sits at a fixed bit
  offset, so reading one means decoding the stream before it, O(n) in at
  most 12,930 payload bits at code 4; (2) **error locality** — a flipped
  payload bit changes every later code, not one field; (3) **canonical
  form** — the encoder emits a strict subset of the strings that decode
  (fixed-width hashes already admit the unwritten top code and the padding
  bits; coded hashes admit far more). None of the three is part of the
  contract that a hash that validates is guaranteed to decode. What replaces
  the fixed offsets as the guarantee is the decoder's totality, which
  `rust/tests/properties.rs` asserts for random byte strings and for coded
  hashes at codes 2–4, and `bitpack.rs`'s unit tests for the coder.
- **Selection on tune (#132).** Fixed before the first `entropy-coded-ac`
  result. For each tier `k` of 2, 3 and 4, separately, from the committed
  tune result: the **one** coded or adaptive arm at code `k` with the lowest
  tune mean ΔE00 among those whose tune `guards (CI)` verdict against the
  config's shipped arm at code `k` is not `FAIL`, scored against that shipped
  arm.
  Under Q13, a tier whose selected arm is not at least 3.0% better than the
  shipped arm on tune is decided in sample, not adopted, with no `holdout3`
  read for it. The register's cap of two holdout arms per entry holds: if
  all three tiers qualify, the two with the largest relative tune gain are
  frozen, Holm is taken across the frozen arms, and the third is decided
  not adopted for want of a holdout read (reopening it takes a new entry).
  Tune tables are reported overall and per stratum, the strata derived from
  each tune image's covariates by `corpus.ts`' `holdout3Strata`; on tune
  they are reported and gate nothing.
- **Sweep.** `sweeps/entropy-coded-ac.json` (#132): corpus `all` with
  `realOnly` (the photographic, alpha and graphics images, without the
  generated fixtures, so the `holdout3` read covers every stratum), the
  shipped layout at each of codes 2–4 as that tier's incumbent, and coded
  and adaptive arms at each tier whose bounds scale the shipped upper-tier
  counts, with `expectBytes` per tier and alpha mode. It is scored through
  the harness's ≤ 100 px encoder input and capped decode, as every tier-3
  and tier-4 measurement in `EXPERIMENTS.md` is; at code 4 that hides any
  coefficient past index 100, so a bound larger than the harness can show
  reads as no better than one it can.
  `entropy-budget.ts` scores only 32 B and 108 B, and codes the stream in
  TypeScript rather than in the format; it stays prior evidence.
- **Frozen comparisons (2026-11-22).** The selection rule above, applied to
  the committed tune result `tools/comparison/results/entropy-coded-ac.json`
  (scored at `d0ddded`, clean; `EXPERIMENTS.md` §13.8), picks
  `t2 adaptive x2` (−3.76%), `t3 coded x3` (−7.58%) and `t4 coded x4`
  (−5.95%), each against its own tier's shipped arm with tune guards `ok` on

  **one Holm family** (m = 2):
  - code 3: `t3 coded x3` against `t3 shipped`;
  - code 4: `t4 coded x4` against `t4 shipped`.

  Code 2 is decided now, not adopted for want of a holdout read (below).
  Each comparison is judged under R1 and the stratum rule, at equal bytes.
  Q8: from each comparison's tune spread (σ_rel 8.44% and 8.27%), the MDE on
  `holdout3`'s 88 images at m = 2 is 2.77% and 2.72%, so R1's 3% is

- **Instrument.** `tools/comparison` at the freeze commit `e3b2eb6` (the

  `76d30226b4f31526133dba59299060e078475aab78ce4bdbe3f602003f18e26a`), which

- **Read, once:** `mise run sweep entropy-coded-ac-frozen --split holdout3

### D2. Embedded/progressive tiers

- **Question.** Should the AC codes of all three channels be interleaved by
  frequency priority, so that a prefix of a code-k hash decodes as a
  lower-quality placeholder of the same image?
- **Class.** Format change. The payload order changes, and a prefix needs a
  defined header and length meaning. Both are required before freeze. At full
  length it is a pure permutation: `EXPERIMENTS.md` §7.11 verified that the
  full-length decode is byte-neutral.
- **Prior evidence (tune only).** §7.11: the first 32 B of an interleaved
  code-2 hash score **+4.15%** ΔE00 against a native code-1 encode, and
  interleaving is worth 5.0% over a channel-sequential prefix. `RATIONALE.md`
  ("Independent (non-embedded) tiers") bounds the value from above: a code-2
  hash decoded at code-1 size scores −20% against native code 1. That bound was
  measured on the pre-2026-08 corpus.
- **Primary metric and threshold.** R2 has two parts here:
  1. **Full length.** Decoded pixels are identical to the shipped
     channel-sequential decode on every image and every tier. The ΔE00
     condition then holds trivially.
  2. **Each prefix cut** (the code-k size taken from a code-(k+1) hash, for
     k = 1, 2, 3). The primary metric is the paired ΔE00 of prefix − native
     code k. Its 95% upper bound must be below the paired gap between native
     code k−1 and native code k, measured in the same run. So a prefix may cost
     at most one tier step. On the tune ladder (`EXPERIMENTS.md` §13.1), the
     gap from code 0 to code 1 is +5.9% (12.156 against 11.481). Guards are
     `ok` against native code k.
- **Tiers.** Codes 1–4, as prefixes of codes 2–4 and as full-length hashes.
- **Sweep.** `sweeps/embedded-tiers.json` exists, for tune. It already has the
  k = 1 cut (32 B of a 108 B hash, plus 48 B and 64 B cuts between) and the
  k = 2 cut (108 B of a 411 B hash, plus a 32 B cut of it), each interleaved,
  with native code-1 and code-2 arms and a full-length interleaved code-3 arm.
  It lacks the k = 3 cut (411 B of a 1623 B hash) and a native code-0 arm,
  which the k = 1 threshold needs. It also has no native code 3 on the shipped
  channel-sequential order, which the k = 3 comparison and the code 2 to code 3
  gap need: its only 411 B arm is interleaved, and treating that as native
  presumes the full-length identity part 1 tests. It needs an
  `embedded-tiers-ladder` config covering the k = 1, 2, 3 cuts, with native
  codes 0–3 encoded in the same run.
- **The order as implemented (#133).** `Tunables::interleave` (off by
  default) writes the fixed-width AC codes merged by frequency priority: each
  code's key is its selection priority `cx² + cy²` (every channel's
  selection is a prefix of one sorted candidate list, so index `i` has the
  same priority in every channel), ties broken by channel (L, a, b, then
  alpha) and then by index. In alpha mode alpha's AC codes join the merge as
  a fourth channel; before #133 alpha mode kept the sequential order, so a
  cut dropped every alpha AC code. The descriptor, aspect, DC and scale
  fields and the alpha DC and scale stay where they are, ahead of the
  payload, so every cut carries all of them and `average_color` is
  unchanged. Each code keeps its width, so the hash length is the shipped
  one. The order applies to the fixed-width fields only: D1's coded stream
  (`ac_coder`) keeps its own channel-sequential order, so if the v0.8
  verdict lands both, a coded tier's prefix is a separate design that this
  entry does not measure.
- **Prefix design (#133), required before freeze.**
  1. **What a prefix is.** The first `L(k, α)` bytes of a code-`j` hash, for
     `1 ≤ k < j ≤ 4`, where `L(k, α)` is the shipped length of a code-`k`
     hash in the same alpha mode: 32, 108 and 411 B opaque, and 32, 103 and
     388 B in alpha mode. Code 0 (21 B) is not a cut: its compact layout is
     its own table, not a prefix of code 1's. A prefix is **not rewritten**.
     It keeps byte 0 as the code-`j` hash wrote it (version, code `j`, the
     alpha flag), because the point of embedding is that a server, a cache
     or a range request can deliver the first bytes of one hash without
     re-encoding it. The header says which hash the bytes came from; the
     length says which cut they are. This entry measures only the cuts one
     code down (`j = k + 1`), which are the ones its criterion names; a
     deeper cut (code 1 of a code-4 hash) follows the same rule but has no
     measurement here, and a prefix mode that accepts one would need its own.
  2. **How validation treats it.** `ChromaHash::from_bytes` is unchanged: it
     requires the exact length the header's code implies, so a prefix is
     rejected with `InvalidLength` as today, and no consumer starts
     accepting a truncated hash by accident. Prefixes are accepted only by
     an **explicit prefix mode**, a separate validating entry point that
     checks the version, the tier code and the reserved bits exactly as
     `from_bytes` does, and accepts a length only if it is `L(j, α)` (the
     whole hash) or one of `L(k, α)` for `1 ≤ k < j`; any other length is
     `InvalidLength`. It stays O(1): at most four lengths, computed from the
     header alone. No new error variant is needed.
  3. **What a decoder does with a cut.** It decodes the code-`j` layout and
     selection the header names, reads every AC code that lies wholly inside
     the prefix, and treats every code that ends past it as the exact-zero
     centre code, which is what the sequential and the interleaved order
     both do with a missing code. It reads no bit past the prefix, so every
     validated prefix decodes: the contract that a hash that validates is
     guaranteed to decode holds for the prefix mode too. Its natural render
     size is code `k`'s, since it carries a code-`k` placeholder; a cap or
     an explicit size applies to it as to any hash.
  `Tunables::cut_tier = k` is that decoder, measured on the full hash: it
  reads only the first `L(k, α)` bytes and renders at code `k`'s natural
  size (`rust/src/decode.rs`). The prefix mode's entry point is not built:
  it is an API change for every language, which the v0.8 verdict lands if
  D2 is adopted.
- **Sweep (#133).** `sweeps/embedded-tiers-ladder.json`, corpus `all` with
  `realOnly` and the `white-black-grey` backdrops, as D1's: the
  photographic, alpha and graphics tune images, so the `holdout3` read covers
  every stratum. Native codes 0–4 on the shipped channel-sequential order
  (`tK native`); the same five codes interleaved at full length, each
  `identicalTo` its native arm, so the run fails if any image decodes to
  other pixels (part 1, read on every image of the run); the three
  interleaved cuts `t2 interleaved cut 1`, `t3 interleaved cut 2` and
  `t4 interleaved cut 3`; and the same three cuts of a channel-sequential

  cuts are reported and judge nothing. Part 1 is also asserted on every encode
  vector at codes 0–4 by `rust/tests/spec_vectors.rs`. Like every tier-3
  and tier-4 measurement, it is scored through the harness's ≤ 100 px

### D4. The separable forward DCT

- **Question.** Should the normative forward transform become the separable
  factorisation (a row pass per distinct `cx`, then a column reduction), for
  the encode speed it buys?
- **Class.** Format change. Spec §10 and §12.6 pin the direct summation.
  Reassociating it can move a coefficient across a quantization boundary, and
  `dct.rs` asserts equality only to 1e-12, not to the bit. It may change no
  byte in practice and would still be a change to the normative text.
- **Prior evidence.** `PERFORMANCE.md` §6, from a committed run: **3.68×** at
  512×512, code 1 (46.70 ms → 12.68 ms), and 2.05× at 100×100. §6 also says
  that no code changed over 40 encodings. No committed test or artifact backs
  that count. Those speedups are chromahash-perf/2 figures: a ratio of two
  one-process cells, each the minimum over its timed blocks. They are not the
  paired /3 statistic the speed criterion below reads, and they stand only as
  prior evidence until a /3 session re-measures them.
- **Primary metric and threshold.** R2 with a speed primary:
  1. **Byte identity, measured.** Encode every image of every corpus and split
     at all five tiers, plus every encode vector in `spec/test-vectors/`, both
     ways. Report the number of hashes that differ. If none do, the ΔE00
     condition holds trivially. If any do, R2's ΔE00 and guard conditions
     apply to those images. The sealed splits are no exception to the seal:
     `holdout3` is encoded for this count only in the entry's one read, after
     the freeze, and `holdout2` and alpha holdout v2 not at all.
  2. **Speed.** The speedup at 512×512 code 1 must be at least **2.0×**,
     measured under `PERFORMANCE.md` §0's chromahash-perf/3 methods: the
     **paired record** for `encode/Rust/t1/512x512/gradient` (base, the direct
     summation) over its `dct_separable` arm, from a **bounded** run
     (`mise run benchmark`, as `mise run benchmark:session` takes it) on a
     host whose committed stability pair passes §0. The base and the arm
     form one unit (`tools/comparison/src/perf/plan.ts`, section E), so
     their processes run back to back, A B on one round and B A on the next,
     and the speedup is the geometric mean of the per-round ratios of
     process minima over complete A B / B A blocks (`pairStats` in
     `tools/comparison/src/perf/probe.ts`). It is not a ratio of two cells'
     reported costs, and it does not come from `--full`, which under /3 is a
     supplement holding only ids the bounded matrix lacks and no lever arm.
     The record must also clear §0's sampling and spread rules: a pair
     `verify:benchmark` withholds (blocks spread past the 10% host bar, or
     fewer complete blocks than the run's processes / 2) gives no reading.
     That is a margin of 1.8× under the prototype's 3.68× before adoption
     stops paying for a version bump. This measurement is
     deferred to a quiet host with the rest of the timing work (#78, #85).
     The amendment below replaces it as the speed condition.
- **The speed criterion, amended (#135), before any count.** The
  maintainer's planning answer for the perf work on this host: "Perf work is
  judged by byte-identity plus load-robust instruction or cycle counts. No
  wall-clock speedup claim is committed." So condition 2 is read in retired
  instructions, not in time:
  1. **Threshold.** Unchanged: at least **2.0×** at 512×512, code 1, on the
     bounded matrix's input for `encode/Rust/t1/512x512/gradient`
     (`makeFixture(512, 512, "gradient")`, `perf/matrix.ts`).
  2. **Statistic.** The ratio of retired user-space instructions per encode,
     incumbent over candidate. One **process pair** measures one arm: two
     processes of `encode_stdin bench-encode 512 512 srgb N`, at `N = 0`
     and `N = 20`, each under `perf stat -x, -e instructions:u,cycles:u`.
     The first encodes once (its warmup), the second 21 times, so
     `(I₂₀ − I₀) / 20` is the per-encode count with process start-up, the
     input read and the warmup cancelled. A count that `perf` reports as
     multiplexed (enabled less than 100% of the time) is refused.
  3. **Pairing.** Each pair of arms is measured in **10 blocks**,
     interleaved A B, B A, A B, …: 5 of each order. A block's ratio is A's
     per-encode count over B's, and the pair's ratio is the **geometric mean
     of the 10 block ratios**. Every block ratio and their range are
     recorded. Cycles (`cycles:u`) are recorded the same way beside it, and
     judge nothing: they move with the host's load, instructions do not.
  4. **Two incumbents.** Pair 1 is the shipped default against
     `dct_separable=1`. Pair 2 is `ACCEL_ALL` (`perf/matrix.ts`, the
     byte-identical levers of `PERFORMANCE.md` §12.1) against `ACCEL_ALL`
     with `dct_separable=1` added, so each pair isolates the transform:
     the separable path replaces `accel_dct_lanes` (`encode.rs`) and every
     other lever applies to both arms. The issue reads the speed against
     the faster of the two incumbents; this entry requires **both** ratios
     to be at least 2.0, which is at least as strict whichever incumbent is
     faster, and records which one is (fewer instructions per encode).
  5. **Reproducibility.** One `mise run dct-instructions` measures both
     pairs twice, back to back. The record gives a reading only if the two
     runs' pair ratios agree within 1% (`|r₁ / r₂ − 1| ≤ 0.01`) for both
     pairs; the reading is the first run's.
  6. **Deferred.** perf/3's paired wall-clock ratio (condition 2 as first
     written) is deferred to a quiet host (#78, #85). It is not part of
     this entry's verdict, and no wall-clock speedup is written into
     `spec/` or `README.md` by it.
- **Byte identity, as measured (#135).** `mise run dct-identity` encodes
  every image with `dct_separable` off (the shipped encoder) and on, and
  counts the hashes that differ, at three sizes, the protocol of
  `mise run lever-identity`:
  1. the harness's encoder input (`loadImage`, at most 100 px on the long

### D5. Downsampling before the transform

- **Question.** Should the encoder downsample the source to a normative size
  before the DCT, so that encode cost stops scaling with the original's pixel
  count?
- **Class.** Format change. The coefficients change, so every hash changes
  (`PERFORMANCE.md` §12.2 item 9). The resampling kernel would become
  normative in every language. `RATIONALE.md` ("Full-resolution encode, no
  pre-downsample") rejected this because the projection is already an exact
  anti-aliased low-pass with no kernel to standardise. Adoption reverses that
  rationale.
- **Prior evidence.** Cost only. Encode is `O(K·W·H)` over the full source:
  46.74 ms at 512×512, code 1 (`PERFORMANCE.md` §3). Nothing has measured the
  quality side.
- **Primary metric and threshold.** R2 with a speed primary, and δ = 1.0% at
  **every** tier, because every hash moves. The speed primary is the encode
  speedup at 1024×1024, code 1, which must be at least **2.0×** on a quiet host.
  It is the same statistic as D4's: the chromahash-perf/3 **paired record** of
  the full-resolution encode cell (base) over the downsampling encode cell
  (arm), the geometric mean of per-round ratios of process minima over
  complete A B / B A blocks, from a bounded run on a host whose committed
  stability pair passes `PERFORMANCE.md` §0, and not withheld by §0's
  sampling or spread rules. It is not a ratio of two cells' reported costs,
  and it does not come from `--full`.
  The source size and the target size are fixed in the entry before freeze.
- **Tiers.** All, codes 0–4. Each tier's ΔE00 is judged separately, and all
  five must pass.
- **Sweep.** It does not exist. It needs a Rust downsampling knob with a named
  kernel and target size, then a `downsample-before-dct` config at codes 0–4.
  The corpus is scored at its full source resolution, not at the ≤100 px
  encoder input the harness uses today, because at 100 px there is almost
  nothing to downsample.
  The speed primary also needs a benchmark arm that does not exist. No
  downsampling arm is in `TUNE_ARMS` (`tools/comparison/src/perf/matrix.ts`),
  and the bounded matrix times the encoder-lever arms only at 100, 256 and
  512 px (`tools/comparison/src/perf/plan.ts`, section E). Under /3 a lever
  arm belongs in the bounded matrix, never in `--full`, which holds no lever
  arm and only ids the bounded matrix lacks. It needs a downsampling encode
  arm at 1024×1024, code 1, and the full-resolution encode cell at the same
  size, added to the bounded matrix as one unit with a planned pair (as
  `levers` in `plan.ts` does for section E), both added before freeze.
  The amendment below replaces that arm as the speed condition, and the
  wall-clock arm is deferred with the rest of the timing work.
- **The amendment (#136), before any `downsample-before-dct` result or
  count.** Everything below is fixed by the commit that records it, and
  every result this entry rests on must record a git rev that descends from
  that commit.
- **The candidates (#136).** `Tunables::downsample` and
  `Tunables::downsample_edge` (`rust/src/encode.rs`, `downsample_dims`,
  `area_spans`, `area_downsample`), off in the shipped encoder and exposed
  by no binding:
  1. **Kernel.** The exact **area (box) average**: each grid cell is the
     integral mean of the source pixels it covers, a pixel straddling two
     cells split between them by its exact coverage. Along an axis of `n`
     source pixels resampled to `m ≤ n` cells, the overlaps are integers in
     units of `1/(n·m)`, so the weights are exact; the sum runs a source row
     at a time into its cells along `x`, then those into the grid along `y`,
     and each cell is divided by its total weight `w·h` last.
  2. **Grid (the target size).** A source of `w × h` pixels is resampled
     only if `w·h > E²`, to `round(w·s) × round(h·s)` with
     `s = E / sqrt(w·h)` (round half away from zero), each axis held to
     `1..=` its source length, so no axis is enlarged; a grid that rounds
     back to `w × h` is no resample. The grid keeps the source's aspect at
     a pixel count of about `E²`, which bounds the transform's cost for any
     aspect. The registered edges are **E ∈ {64, 128, 256, 512}**.
  3. **Placement** (`downsample`):
     - **`linear`** averages the source's linear-light RGB, premultiplied
       by alpha, and its alpha (an exact integer sum, so an opaque cell is
       exactly 1 and the hash's alpha mode is the source's) straight off the
       bytes, and runs the colour pass, the alpha average and the composite
       on the grid. It saves the colour pass as well as the transform.
     - **`oklab`** runs the shipped colour pass and composite at full
       resolution and averages the composited OKLAB planes (and the alpha
       plane, in alpha mode) onto the grid. The composite is linear in each
       pixel, so this is the area average of exactly what the
       full-resolution transform reads, and only the transform moves.
  4. **The hash's aspect** is always the source's, so the selection order
     and the decoder are unchanged: the decode path takes nothing new, and
     only the coefficients move.

  A candidate is one placement and one edge: 8 candidates.

### D9. Chroma-from-luma, adaptive or conditional

- **Question.** Does a chroma-from-luma predictor pay for itself in an
  adaptive form (the encoder decides per image, and signals the decision) or
  a conditional one (active only where the decoder can tell from the hash
  that it is, such as a tier code), at any tier?
- **Class.** Format change.
- **Why a new entry.** D7 decided the uniform signalled predictor, and says
  reopening it takes a new entry with a cheaper way to signal the gains. An
  adaptive or conditional form is such a way: a conditional form spends the
  field only where the prediction is largest, and an adaptive one only on
  the images it helps.
- **Prior evidence (tune only).** As D7: the free predictor, which bounds any
  form that signals gains from above, is −0.10% at code 1, −0.45% at code 3
  and −0.90% at code 4 (`EXPERIMENTS.md` §7.10), and no tier reaches R1's 3%.
  A per-image form is bounded instead by the better of the free predictor and
  no predictor on each image, which nothing has measured. That bound is an
  oracle and cannot itself be an arm (the adaptive/conditional rule).
- **Primary metric and threshold.** R1 at equal bytes against the shipped
  layout of each tier, each tier decided separately, scored as deployed and
  under the stratum rule. A conditional form names its activation class
  before its tune sweep.
- **Tiers.** Codes 0–4; a conditional form is judged at the tiers it is
  active in.
- **Selection on tune.** The default, with at most two arms across the
  entry. Under Q13 the entry is decided in sample, with no `holdout3` read,
  if no as-deployed arm is at least 3% better than the shipped layout on
  tune. The per-image oracle bound is measured on tune first: if it is under
  3% at a tier, no deployed form can pass there.
- **Sweep.** It does not exist. It needs the encoder's per-image decision, or
  the activation class, behind a `Tunables` knob, with the signalling paid
  for inside the tier's bytes, and a `cfl-adaptive` config at codes 0–4 with
  `expectBytes` per tier. `cfl-probe.ts` measures the free and signalled
  predictors today and has no per-image decision.
- **Status:** open. Criteria approved: 2026-11-22 at the commit of #152
  ("The approval, 2026-11-22"). Holdout arms: none; `holdout3` has not been
  read for this entry. Verdict: none.

1. **The SSIMULACRA2 guard against the sample size** (`EXPERIMENTS.md` §13.5,
   point 3). At n ≈ 31, the paired SSIMULACRA2 interval of a near-null arm is
   several points wide, against a 1.0-point tolerance, so such an arm can never
   be `ok`. **Default:** keep the tolerance, and require `ok`. An arm that
   clears R1's 3% usually moves SSIMULACRA2 decisively. §13.5 found every arm
   §4.5 and §7.12 adopted `ok` on intervals. The default bites on R2 entries,
   where the arm is near-null by design. **Alternatives:** widen the tolerance
   for R2 only, to a stated value; or accept `inconclusive` on SSIMULACRA2 for
   R2, with disclosure.
2. **SSIMULACRA2 for D3.** **Default:** keep it as a guard, so the window
   probably fails as it did at code 2. **Alternative:** exempt it for D3 alone,
   on the U19 argument in §12.3. That changes a guard for one candidate, and the
   approval would say so explicitly.
3. **The non-inferiority margin δ.** **Default:** 1.0% of the incumbent's mean
   ΔE00, for D2's full-length check, D3, D4 and D5. The nearest precedent is
   the +0.45% `RATIONALE.md` accepted for the descriptor byte, which was a
   payment it recorded rather than a margin it set.
4. **Multiplicity across entries.** **Default:** Holm within each entry's
   holdout arms only. The entries are separate questions with separate actions,
   and each reads its split once. **Alternative:** Holm across every holdout
   test in the register. That is stricter, and it grows with the number of
   entries.
5. **The speed thresholds for D4 and D5.** **Default:** at least 2.0× on a
   quiet host, measured at 512×512 for D4 and at 1024×1024 for D5, as the
   chromahash-perf/3 paired record from a bounded run: the geometric mean of
   per-round ratios of process minima over complete A B / B A blocks
   (`PERFORMANCE.md` §0), not a ratio of cells and not from `--full`.
6. **The prefix bound for D2.** **Default:** a prefix may cost at most one tier
   step, measured in the same run.
7. **The confirmations.** **Default:** C1 and C2 are registered, so
   `holdout2` and alpha holdout v2 can open for them. Without them the
   shipped `sel_hv = 0.15` never receives out-of-sample evidence.

- **Q4. Holm within each entry's holdout arms.** C1 and C2 are separate

- **Q7. C1 and C2 are registered.**
