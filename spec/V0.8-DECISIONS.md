# ChromaHash v0.8 Decision Register

What v0.8.0 has to decide, and what evidence decides each question. Every
criterion is written down here **before** the experiment that answers it runs,
so no result can choose its own criterion. [`EXPERIMENTS.md`](EXPERIMENTS.md) is
the measurement log, [`RATIONALE.md`](RATIONALE.md) records why the shipped
constants are what they are, and [`PERFORMANCE.md`](PERFORMANCE.md) covers
compute cost. This file adds no measurements. It cites them, and it sets the
rules for the ones still to come.

> **Status: draft. No criterion here has been approved.** Every entry except
> D7 is `open`. The metrics, thresholds and sweeps below are **proposals**
> taken from the documents cited. They become binding only when the user
> approves them and the entry records that approval (see "Statuses"). Until
> then no experiment may run against them, whether on tune or on a holdout.
> The questions at the end of this file are the choices that approval has to
> make.

Tier numbers are the **shipped codes** throughout: `0` = compact (21 B),
`1` = default (32 B), `2` = 108 B, `3` = 411 B, `4` = 1623 B. Most sections of
`EXPERIMENTS.md` use the older numbering, where "tier 0" means code 1 (see the
note at its head). Figures quoted from those sections are converted here.

## How the register works

### Statuses

Each entry has exactly one status, and the `Status` column of the register
table is its record. The holdout gates that #76 (`holdout2`) and #93 (the new
alpha split) are to build read that column, by entry ID. Keep the column and the
three status words exactly as they are written here.

| status | meaning | what may run |
|---|---|---|
| `open` | The criteria are drafted, or approved while the holdout arms are not yet fixed. | Nothing, until the entry's `Criteria approved` field is filled. After that, the entry's tune sweep. |
| `frozen` | The criteria are approved, and the arms to be scored on the holdout are fixed from the tune result by the entry's own selection rule. | The entry reads its holdout **once**, with exactly the recorded arms. |
| `decided` | The verdict is recorded, with the committed result it rests on. | Nothing further under this entry. Reopening it takes a new entry. |

An entry moves forward only, `open` → `frozen` → `decided`. Two moves need the
user: filling `Criteria approved`, and the move to `frozen`, which the user
approves by approving the holdout arm list. The move to `decided` is mechanical.
It applies the frozen rule to the committed holdout result, and involves no
further judgement.

Freezing an entry also pins its instrument. The freeze records the commit SHA
whose `tools/comparison` scores the holdout, and the sweep config file. The
holdout result is committed under `tools/comparison/results/` with the
provenance #73 added (git rev, corpus digests, `iqa-cli` version). A tooling
change after the freeze does not apply to a frozen entry. Two examples are the
interval resample count #88 was to raise (`stats.ts` still draws intervals at
1,000) and a change to a guard tolerance.

### Change classes

| class | what moves | release |
|---|---|---|
| **byte-identical** | Nothing: the same hash and the same decoded pixels. | A patch release. `spec/test-vectors/` and `mise run rd:gate` are sufficient evidence (`PERFORMANCE.md` §12.1). |
| **decode change** | The decoded pixels and the decode vectors. No hash byte changes. | A format version: the decoder is normative, so every language lands together. |
| **format change** | The hash bytes, and so every encode vector. | A format version: a version bump, regenerated vectors, and all nine languages together. |

### Evidence splits

- **tune**: the photographic tune split, into which #76 folds the spent
  32-image holdout. Candidates are chosen here.
- **`holdout2`**: the sealed photographic split #76 curates. It is not read at
  all until an entry is `frozen`, and then once per entry.
- **alpha holdout v2**: the sealed alpha split #93 curates. It replaces the
  split retired in #83. Same rules.
- **graphics**: the graphic corpus. It is reported as secondary evidence for
  every format change and gates nothing.

The old photographic holdout does not count as out-of-sample evidence for any
entry here. `EXPERIMENTS.md` §11.12 records that it informed a decision in
every round, and that `sel_hv = 0.15` was selected on it.

### The decision rules

Each entry names one rule, and names which parameters it changes. Rules are
evaluated by the committed sweep tooling (`sweep.ts`, `arms-core.ts`,
`stats.ts`). Guard tolerances are the ones `sweep.ts` applies: SSIMULACRA2 may
drop at most 1.0 point (absolute), and Butteraugli and DSSIM may rise at most
2% (relative).

**R1: superiority.** This is `RATIONALE.md`'s pre-registered retune rule,
tightened to the statistics of `EXPERIMENTS.md` §13.5. An arm passes against
its incumbent, on the entry's holdout, only if all four of these hold:

1. Its mean ΔE00 is at least **3.0%** lower (point estimate).
2. The paired ΔE00 difference is significant at 0.05 **after Holm** across
   the holdout arms of that entry.
3. Every guard is **`ok` on the paired intervals** (`guards (CI)`). A guard
   that is `inconclusive` fails.
4. The verdict does not change under `mise run arms --seed-sensitivity`.
   §13.5 measured 14 Holm verdicts that change with the seed.

**R2: non-inferiority.** For candidates whose benefit is not ΔE00: speed,
decode flexibility, or artifacts. An arm passes, on the entry's holdout, if:

1. The upper bound of the paired 95% interval of mean ΔE00 (arm − incumbent)
   is at most **+δ**, where δ is **1.0%** of the incumbent's mean unless the
   entry sets it otherwise.
2. Every guard is `ok` on intervals, as in R1.
3. The entry's own primary metric passes its own threshold.
4. Condition 4 of R1.

**Selection on tune.** Each entry fixes, before its tune sweep runs, how the
holdout arms are chosen from the tune result. The default is **one arm**: the
tune arm with the best primary metric among those whose tune `guards (CI)`
verdict is not `FAIL`, scored against the incumbent. Taking at most two arms
keeps the Holm family on the holdout small. Holm is still applied across
whatever arms are recorded.

### D1. Entropy-coded AC

- **Question.** Does coding the AC codes with an adaptive entropy coder, and
  spending the bits it saves on more coefficients, beat the shipped fixed-width
  layout at equal bytes by enough to pay for the loss of the O(1) length check?
- **Class.** Format change. The length formula stops being the validity check,
  so `ChromaHash::from_bytes` needs a different guarantee that a validated hash
  decodes. An approved design for that guarantee is **required before
  freeze**: the metric cannot price the loss of the check, so passing R1 is
  necessary for adoption, but not sufficient.
- **Prior evidence (tune only).** `EXPERIMENTS.md` §7.13, with the coder scored
  leave-one-image-out: −7.4% of the AC bits at 32 B, which buys **−1.6% ΔE00**
  at code 1 and **−4.8%** at code 2. Both are against the best fixed-field
  layout at the same budget, not the shipped one. `RATIONALE.md` ("No entropy
  coding") says the trade reverses at codes 3–4, where there are 3–13 kbit, but
  nothing has measured those codes.
- **Primary metric and threshold.** R1, at equal bytes against the shipped
  layout of each tier. Each tier is decided separately.
- **Tiers.** Codes 2, 3 and 4. Code 1 is excluded because §7.13's tune estimate
  (−1.6%) is below R1's 3% before any holdout shrinkage. Code 0 is excluded
  too: its AC payload is smaller still, and §7.13 finds that the saving shrinks
  with the payload (a table-free coder loses to the static entropy at code 1's
  44 symbols).
- **Sweep.** It does not exist. It needs a Rust coder behind a `Tunables` knob,
  and an `entropy-coded-ac` sweep config with the coder's layouts at codes 2–4
  and `expectBytes` per tier. `entropy-budget.ts` today scores only 32 B and
  108 B, and codes the stream in TypeScript rather than in the format.
- **Status.** `open`. Criteria approved: —. Holdout arms: —. Verdict: —.

### D2. Embedded/progressive tiers

- **Question.** Should the AC codes of all three channels be interleaved by
  frequency priority, so that a prefix of a code-k hash decodes as a
  lower-quality placeholder of the same image?
- **Class.** Format change. The payload order changes, and a prefix needs a
  defined header and length meaning. Both are required before freeze. At full
  length it is a pure permutation: `EXPERIMENTS.md` §7.11 verified that the
  full-length decode is byte-neutral.
- **Prior evidence (tune only).** §7.11: the first 32 B of an interleaved
  code-2 hash score **+4.21%** ΔE00 against a native code-1 encode, and
  interleaving is worth 5.0% over a channel-sequential prefix. `RATIONALE.md`
  ("Independent (non-embedded) tiers") bounds the value from above: a code-2
  hash decoded at code-1 size scores −20% against native code 1. That bound was
  measured on the pre-2026-08 corpus.
- **Primary metric and threshold.** R2 has two parts here:
  1. **Full length.** Decoded pixels are identical to the shipped
     channel-sequential decode on every image and every tier. The ΔE00
     condition then holds trivially.
  2. **Each prefix cut** (the code-k size taken from a code-(k+1) hash, for
     k = 1, 2, 3). The primary metric is the paired ΔE00 of prefix − native
     code k. Its 95% upper bound must be below the paired gap between native
     code k−1 and native code k, measured in the same run. So a prefix may cost
     at most one tier step. On the tune ladder (`EXPERIMENTS.md` §13.1), the
     gap from code 0 to code 1 is +5.9% (12.147 against 11.473). Guards are
     `ok` against native code k.
- **Tiers.** Codes 1–4, as prefixes of codes 2–4 and as full-length hashes.
- **Sweep.** `sweeps/embedded-tiers.json` exists, for tune. It already has the
  k = 1 cut (32 B of a 108 B hash, plus 48 B and 64 B cuts between) and the
  k = 2 cut (108 B of a 411 B hash, plus a 32 B cut of it), each interleaved,
  with native code-1 and code-2 arms and a full-length interleaved code-3 arm.
  It lacks the k = 3 cut (411 B of a 1623 B hash) and a native code-0 arm,
  which the k = 1 threshold needs. It also has no native code 3 on the shipped
  channel-sequential order, which the k = 3 comparison and the code 2 to code 3
  gap need: its only 411 B arm is interleaved, and treating that as native
  presumes the full-length identity part 1 tests. It needs an
  `embedded-tiers-ladder` config covering the k = 1, 2, 3 cuts, with native
  codes 0–3 encoded in the same run.
- **Status.** `open`. Criteria approved: —. Holdout arms: —. Verdict: —.

### D3. The synthesis window at codes 3–4

- **Question.** Does a decoder-side synthesis taper remove the invented
  structure codes 3–4 add, at no ΔE00 cost?
- **Class.** Decode change. The encoder is byte-identical with the window on
  (`EXPERIMENTS.md` §12.2).
- **Prior evidence.** §12.2–§12.3 measured the window only at codes 1 and 2.
  There it removes up to 73% of spurious detail (at code 1), and every arm
  that removes any fails guards. The one arm that passes, `chroma only`
  (`ok`/`ok` at code 1, `ok` on means and `inconclusive` on intervals at
  code 2), passes because it changes nothing: its spurious detail is 3.58
  and 3.71 against shipped 3.53 and 3.65.
  At code 2, `w_min 0.85` costs +0.06% ΔE00, with a paired interval straddling
  zero, and fails only SSIMULACRA2 (−3.2 against a tolerance of −1.0). §13.2
  finds that codes 3–4 invent more than code 2, on the 32 px pinned grid: code
  3 − code 2 is +0.717 [+0.334, +1.136], and code 4 − code 2 is +0.735
  [+0.261, +1.177]. §13.4 records that the window was never measured where that
  rise is.
- **Primary metric and threshold.** R1 cannot pass here, because the window
  raises ΔE00 by construction. Holding it to R1 decides the entry before it
  runs. So R2 applies, with an artifact primary:
  - The primary metric is spurious detail on the 32 px pinned grid
    (`artifact-ladder-common-grid`'s instrument). The upper bound of the
    paired 95% interval of (window arm at code k) − (shipped code 2) must be
    at most 0. In other words, the window must bring code k back to the floor
    §13.2 measured at codes 1–2.
  - ΔE00 must be non-inferior with δ = 1.0%. Guards follow R2, and so do
    SSIMULACRA2 and its tolerance, unless Q2 below changes them. Under the
    guards as they stand, §12.3's SSIMULACRA2 failure at code 2 is the likely
    outcome here too.
- **Tiers.** Codes 3 and 4, each decided separately.
- **Sweep.** It does not exist. It needs a `synthesis-window-codes34` config:
  the `synthesis-window-upper` arms at `tier: 3` and `tier: 4`, all four window
  knobs pinned in every arm, `artifacts: true`, and the spectral grid pinned to
  32 px. It also needs a shipped code-2 arm for the primary comparison, which
  therefore spans two byte counts; the entry records that.
- **Status.** `open`. Criteria approved: —. Holdout arms: —. Verdict: —.

### D4. The separable forward DCT

- **Question.** Should the normative forward transform become the separable
  factorisation (a row pass per distinct `cx`, then a column reduction), for
  the encode speed it buys?
- **Class.** Format change. Spec §10 and §12.6 pin the direct summation.
  Reassociating it can move a coefficient across a quantization boundary, and
  `dct.rs` asserts equality only to 1e-12, not to the bit. It may change no
  byte in practice and would still be a change to the normative text.
- **Prior evidence.** `PERFORMANCE.md` §6, from a committed run: **3.68×** at
  512×512, code 1 (46.70 ms → 12.68 ms), and 2.05× at 100×100. §6 also says
  that no code changed over 40 encodings. No committed test or artifact backs
  that count.
- **Primary metric and threshold.** R2 with a speed primary:
  1. **Byte identity, measured.** Encode every image of every corpus and split
     at all five tiers, plus every encode vector in `spec/test-vectors/`, both
     ways. Report the number of hashes that differ. If none do, the ΔE00
     condition holds trivially. If any do, R2's ΔE00 and guard conditions
     apply to those images.
  2. **Speed.** The speedup at 512×512 code 1 must be at least **2.0×**, from
     `mise run benchmark:full` on a host that passes `PERFORMANCE.md` §0's
     stability rule. The speedup is the direct cell's reported cost divided by
     the separable cell's, where each cell's reported cost is the minimum over
     its timed blocks (`nsPerOp` in `tools/comparison/src/perf/probe.ts`), the
     statistic `benchmark:full` reports and `PERFORMANCE.md` §6's 3.68× is.
     That is a margin of 1.8× under the prototype's 3.68× before adoption
     stops paying for a version bump. This measurement is
     deferred to a quiet host with the rest of the timing work (#78, #85).
- **Tiers.** All, codes 0–4.
- **Sweep.** The byte-identity count needs no sweep: `Tunables::dct_separable`
  exists and `CHROMAHASH_TUNE` can set it. A committed script to count the
  differing hashes is needed. Speed comes from `benchmark:full`.
- **Status.** `open`. Criteria approved: —. Holdout arms: —. Verdict: —.

### D5. Downsampling before the transform

- **Question.** Should the encoder downsample the source to a normative size
  before the DCT, so that encode cost stops scaling with the original's pixel
  count?
- **Class.** Format change. The coefficients change, so every hash changes
  (`PERFORMANCE.md` §12.2 item 9). The resampling kernel would become
  normative in every language. `RATIONALE.md` ("Full-resolution encode, no
  pre-downsample") rejected this because the projection is already an exact
  anti-aliased low-pass with no kernel to standardise. Adoption reverses that
  rationale.
- **Prior evidence.** Cost only. Encode is `O(K·W·H)` over the full source:
  46.74 ms at 512×512, code 1 (`PERFORMANCE.md` §3). Nothing has measured the
  quality side.
- **Primary metric and threshold.** R2 with a speed primary, and δ = 1.0% at
  **every** tier, because every hash moves. The speed primary is the encode
  speedup at 1024×1024, code 1, which must be at least **2.0×** on a quiet host.
  It is the same statistic as D4's: the full-resolution encode cell's reported
  cost divided by the downsampling encode cell's, each the minimum over its
  timed blocks, from `mise run benchmark:full`.
  The source size and the target size are fixed in the entry before freeze.
- **Tiers.** All, codes 0–4. Each tier's ΔE00 is judged separately, and all
  five must pass.
- **Sweep.** It does not exist. It needs a Rust downsampling knob with a named
  kernel and target size, then a `downsample-before-dct` config at codes 0–4.
  The corpus is scored at its full source resolution, not at the ≤100 px
  encoder input the harness uses today, because at 100 px there is almost
  nothing to downsample.
- **Status.** `open`. Criteria approved: —. Holdout arms: —. Verdict: —.

### D6. The alpha allocation at codes 3–4

- **Question.** Codes 2–4 share one base row, scaled by `4^(level)`. Is that
  row still right at codes 3 and 4, or do those tiers want a different alpha
  and chroma split?
- **Class.** Format change for alpha-mode hashes at codes 3–4. A per-tier row
  would also break the single-row scaling structure, and the entry records that
  cost alongside the metric.
- **Prior evidence (tune only).** `EXPERIMENTS.md` §11.11 measured the tier-0
  choice (`A28@3 L22@4 C3@3`) at code 2. There it is within 0.06% of the best
  equal-budget arm. Codes 3–4 inherit it without a measurement:
  `RATIONALE.md`'s future work item 2 lists this as still open.
- **Primary metric and threshold.** R1 on alpha holdout v2 (#93), against the
  shipped scaled row at the same code. The alpha AC counts must stay monotone
  across tiers, which `validate.py` asserts.
- **Tiers.** Codes 3 and 4, each decided separately.
- **Sweep.** It does not exist. It needs an `alpha-tiers34` config: §11.11's
  equal-budget arms at `tier: 3` and `tier: 4`, with `expectBytes` for each.
- **Status.** `open`. Criteria approved: —. Holdout arms: —. Verdict: —.
  Freezing this entry depends on #93.

### D7. Chroma-from-luma (U14)

- **Question.** Does a signalled per-channel luma-to-chroma predictor pay for
  its gain field at any tier?
- **Class.** Format change.
- **Evidence.** `EXPERIMENTS.md` §4.8 and §7.10, and `RATIONALE.md` future
  work item 1. Paid for at 32 B, it is **+2.18%** against the shipped layout.
  The free predictor, whose gains are not paid for, is −0.09% at code 1,
  −0.45% at code 3 and **−0.90% at code 4**. The free arm gives the predictor
  away at no bit cost, so it bounds any signalled form from above. §7.10 also
  shows that gain precision does not matter (`cfl-range`). No tier comes within
  reach of R1's 3%.
- **Status.** `decided`: not a v0.8 change. The verdict rests on tune alone. It
  needs no holdout read, because the upper bound (the free predictor) fails R1
  in sample. Reopening it takes a new entry with a cheaper way to signal the
  gains, which is what §7.10 names as the only open route.

## The ceiling on every artifact claim: U19

No metric in this repository has been validated against human judgement at
placeholder fidelity (`EXPERIMENTS.md` §7.14, §12.5 and §13.4; `RATIONALE.md`
future work item 11). This affects every entry in three ways:

- Every verdict above is a **metric** verdict. It is necessary for a change,
  and it is not evidence that a viewer prefers the result.
- D3 is where this matters most. Its outcome turns on whether SSIMULACRA2, or
  the spurious-detail metric, is right about placeholders. Nothing in this
  repository can say which.
- The register cannot settle U19. The only thing that can is a human study,
  which is outside the repository. It stays listed here, with no status, so
  that no verdict is read as having settled it.

## Questions the approval has to answer

Each question has a proposed default, which the entries above already assume,
and names what the answer changes.

1. **The SSIMULACRA2 guard against the sample size** (`EXPERIMENTS.md` §13.5,
   point 3). At n ≈ 31, the paired SSIMULACRA2 interval of a near-null arm is
   several points wide, against a 1.0-point tolerance, so such an arm can never
   be `ok`. **Default:** keep the tolerance, and require `ok`. An arm that
   clears R1's 3% usually moves SSIMULACRA2 decisively. §13.5 found every arm
   §4.5 and §7.12 adopted `ok` on intervals. The default bites on R2 entries,
   where the arm is near-null by design. **Alternatives:** widen the tolerance
   for R2 only, to a stated value; or accept `inconclusive` on SSIMULACRA2 for
   R2, with disclosure.
2. **SSIMULACRA2 for D3.** **Default:** keep it as a guard, so the window
   probably fails as it did at code 2. **Alternative:** exempt it for D3 alone,
   on the U19 argument in §12.3. That changes a guard for one candidate, and the
   approval would say so explicitly.
3. **The non-inferiority margin δ.** **Default:** 1.0% of the incumbent's mean
   ΔE00, for D2's full-length check, D3, D4 and D5. The nearest precedent is
   the +0.45% `RATIONALE.md` accepted for the descriptor byte, which was a
   payment it recorded rather than a margin it set.
4. **Multiplicity across entries.** **Default:** Holm within each entry's
   holdout arms only. The entries are separate questions with separate actions,
   and each reads its split once. **Alternative:** Holm across every holdout
   test in the register. That is stricter, and it grows with the number of
   entries.
5. **The speed thresholds for D4 and D5.** **Default:** at least 2.0× on a
